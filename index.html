About

I like to build apps that people find useful and can draw insight from, the kind that have some machine learning or mathematical element to them. The projects I keep returning to are the ones a person can open and actually use. One is a retrieval-augmented generation stack that serves fine-tuned models with vLLM and TensorRT-LLM. Another is a transcription tool that streams who said what from a browser tab a few seconds behind live. Another is a reinforcement-learning harness that teaches itself to play arbitrary games from self-play. Machine learning runs through most of my work, but the model is rarely the interesting part on its own, and what really matters is the application built around it. Most of what I build combines existing technology into something more useful than its parts, wiring pretrained models to retrieval over embeddings and giving applications a persistent memory in an ordinary database so they get better the longer you use them.

My interest in this kind of work goes back to school. I studied electrical engineering at UT Austin and then spent three years in computer science at Stanford, and the timing there mattered a great deal. Transformer models were just emerging, and I focused my coursework on them right as language, vision, audio, and speech were all converging on the same architecture. Since then I have worked at both large companies and startups, building models across all of those domains, from small networks up to large transformers, and I care as much about getting them to run well in production as I do about training them in the first place.

That range is why the work spans both ends of the stack. At one end I fully fine-tune 8B-parameter models across several GPUs, quantize them down to FP8, and serve them. At the other end I design the interface, the schema, and the deployment for the app a person actually touches. Evaluation is what ties the two ends together, whether that means a calibrated judge for model outputs or a careful benchmark for a retrieval pipeline, because honest measurement is what separates a real product from a demo.

Outside of work I am usually building apps and design projects for their own sake. If you'd like to talk or work together, feel free to reach out.

Selected work

A few projects with full write-ups. Each page walks through the decisions step by step, with the measurements that back them.

Speaker-attributed transcription

Joins Whisper with a speaker-diarization pipeline assembled from open parts, scored end to end with WER and DER written from scratch, and streams speaker-labeled words from a browser tab about five seconds behind live.

Whisper CTranslate2 Diarization

An 8B Rust coder, fully fine-tuned

A full BF16 fine-tune of Qwen3-8B for idiomatic Rust on four H100s, quantized to FP8 and served with vLLM, then improved further by reinforcement learning with the compiler as the reward.

Qwen3-8B FSDP2 vLLM

Credit risk with a provable audit

An 800,000-application simulated portfolio with bias planted deliberately, so the fair-lending audit can be tested against ground truth. It has to catch the redlining proxy, and it does, rejecting the challenger model that had the best Gini.

LightGBM SHAP Fair lending

A general game-playing RL harness

One reinforcement-learning framework that learns to play arbitrary turn-based games from self-play alone. Any game plugs into a shared environment interface, an AlphaZero-style policy-value network guides the search with Monte Carlo tree search, and the same training loop transfers from one game to the next with no game-specific heuristics.

Self-play RL MCTS PyTorch

Retrieval and served inference, optimized

A retrieval-augmented generation stack over vector embeddings and approximate nearest-neighbor search, wired to language models served with vLLM and TensorRT-LLM and quantized to FP8, with throughput and latency measured against the accuracy each optimization costs.

RAG & embeddings vLLM / TensorRT-LLM FP8 serving

Robot imitation learning

A 262M-parameter diffusion policy trained from scratch on PushT and evaluated on matched seeds against the published reference, building out a success-versus-compute scaling curve.

Diffusion policy LeRobot PyTorch
